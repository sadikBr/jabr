With drag the ball falls short, its path is no longer a parabola, and there
is **no formula** for where it lands. For your program that is not a problem
at all. The simulation just needs a different acceleration.

### Drag as a vector

Drag acts opposite to the velocity, with a size that grows as the speed
squared. Writing $s = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}$ for the speed and $k$ for the
drag constant divided by the mass, the acceleration it causes is

$$
a_x = -k\,s\,v_x \qquad\qquad a_y = -k\,s\,v_y
$$

Each component is slowed in proportion to itself, so the push is always
straight back along the direction of travel. Gravity still adds its $-g$ to
$a_y$.

So the first step of the loop changes. Instead of only gravity changing `vy`,
both velocity components change by their acceleration times `dt`.

### Your task

Replace the two acceleration lines in `landing_distance` so they include
drag. Nothing else needs to change. In particular, `aim` is exactly the
function you wrote in the last milestone, with the drag passed along. Run the
program and compare the angle needed with and without air.
