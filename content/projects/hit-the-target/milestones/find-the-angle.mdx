You have a function that says where a throw lands. Now turn the question
round: given where you _want_ it to land, what angle do you need?

Think about what happens as the angle goes up from 0° towards 40°: the ball
lands further and further away. So for any target within reach there is
exactly one angle in that range that hits it. Too low, and the ball falls
short. Too high, and it flies long.

That is the situation the bisection method was made for. You met it in the
Continuity lesson, trapping the root of an equation. Here you trap an angle:

1. Start with a range you know contains the answer: 0° to 40°.
2. Try the angle in the **middle**.
3. If that throw falls **short**, the answer is in the upper half. If it
   flies **long**, the lower half.
4. Keep the half that contains the answer, and repeat until the range is
   tiny.

Each try halves the range. Twelve tries narrow 40° down to a hundredth of a
degree.

<Callout kind="insight" title="You do not need to know how the function works">

Bisection never looks inside `landing_distance`. It only asks "too short or
too long?". That is why the same search will still work in the next
milestone, when the physics inside changes.

</Callout>

### Your task

Complete the two lines in `aim` that keep the correct half of the range. The
program then aims at three targets and checks each answer by firing.
