Every simulation you have ever seen, from a weather forecast to a video game,
runs on the same loop:

1. Look at how fast things are changing **right now**.
2. Move everything forward by a tiny slice of time.
3. Repeat.

You are going to build a rocket simulator on that loop, one piece at a time.
Start with the simplest thing that moves: a point travelling in a straight
line at a constant velocity $v$.

In a slice of time $dt$, it covers a distance

$$
\Delta x = v \, dt
$$

So if it starts at $x = 0$ and you take one step after another, its positions
are $0,\; v\,dt,\; 2v\,dt,\; 3v\,dt, \dots$

<Callout kind="insight" title="This is calculus, backwards">

Velocity is the derivative of position: it tells you how fast position is
changing. The loop does the reverse. It takes the rate of change and rebuilds
the position from it, one small step at a time.

</Callout>
