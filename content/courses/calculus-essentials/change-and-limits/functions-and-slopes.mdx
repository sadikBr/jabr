<Step kind="see" title="A rule, and its picture">

A taxi charges €3 the moment you get in, and then €2 for every kilometre.
Tell me how far you are going and I can tell you the fare: 5 km costs
$2 \times 5 + 3 = 13$ euros.

That is a **function**: a rule that takes an input (the distance) and gives
back exactly one output (the fare). We give the rule a name, say $f$, and
write

$$
f(x) = 2x + 3
$$

Read $f(x)$ as "$f$ of $x$": the output when the input is $x$. So $f(5) = 13$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Function">

A **function** is a rule that assigns to each input exactly one output.

Its **graph** is the picture you get by plotting every input along the
horizontal axis and the matching output up the vertical axis.

</Callout>

The graph of the taxi fare is a straight line. Here it is, with two points
marked on it. Move them anywhere you like and watch the three numbers
underneath.

The run and the rise change as you move the points. Their ratio never does:
it is always 2 euros per kilometre. That number is the **slope** of the line,
and it is exactly the "€2 for every kilometre" in the fare.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Slope of a line">

The **slope** of a line is the change in height divided by the change in
horizontal position between any two of its points:

$$
\text{slope} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
$$

- A **positive** slope goes uphill from left to right.
- A **negative** slope goes downhill.
- A slope of **zero** is flat.

A line with slope $m$ that crosses the vertical axis at height $c$ has the
equation $y = mx + c$.

</Callout>

A straight line is the _only_ kind of graph where the slope is the same
everywhere. Try the same thing on a curve, and the answer depends on which
two points you choose.

</Step>

that rate is always the same. For most things in the world it is not: a
