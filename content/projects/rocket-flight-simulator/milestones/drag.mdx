### The drag force

Air resistance grows quickly with speed. For something moving fast through
air, a good model is

$$
F_\text{drag} = k\,v^2
$$

where $k$ bundles together the air's density and the object's size and shape.
Double the speed and the drag is four times as large.

The important part is its **direction**: drag always pushes against the
motion. On the way up it points down, adding to gravity. On the way down it
points up, fighting gravity.

### Terminal velocity

That second case has a striking consequence. As a falling object speeds up,
drag grows until it exactly balances the weight. Then the net force is zero
and the object stops accelerating. It falls at a steady **terminal velocity**:

$$
k\,v_t^2 = m\,g \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_t = \sqrt{\frac{m\,g}{k}}
$$

For your empty rocket ($m = 1\ \text{kg}$, $k = 0.002\ \text{kg/m}$) that is
about $70\ \text{m/s}$. However high it flies, it will not come down faster.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Watch the sign">

`v**2` is positive whether the rocket is going up or down, so it cannot tell
you which way the force points. You need an expression that has the size of
$v^2$ and the sign of $v$.

</Callout>
