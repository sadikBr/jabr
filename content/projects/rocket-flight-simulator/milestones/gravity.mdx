In the last milestone the velocity never changed. Real things speed up and
slow down. Drop something and watch how it falls:

<Interactive id="drop-test" props={{ place: "earth", air: false }} />

Look at the trail of dots, one every tenth of a second. The gaps get wider
on the way down: the object covers more distance in each tenth of a second
than in the one before. It is **accelerating**.

Near the ground, gravity gives everything the same downward acceleration,
$g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. So now two things change in every time step:

$$
y \leftarrow y + v\,dt \qquad\qquad v \leftarrow v - g\,dt
$$

The height moves by the velocity, and the velocity moves by the acceleration.
Updating each quantity by (its rate of change) × $dt$ is called **Euler's
method**.

### How good is it?

For constant gravity there is an exact answer to compare with:

$$
y(t) = y_0 + v_0\,t - \tfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2
$$

Euler's method does not know this formula. It treats the velocity as constant
during each step, which is not quite true, so it drifts a little. The smaller
the step, the smaller the drift.

### Your task

Write the exact formula in `exact_height`, then the three update lines in
`fall`. The program prints both answers for three step sizes, so you can see
the error shrink.
