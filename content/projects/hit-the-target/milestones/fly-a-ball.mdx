In this project you will write a program that finds the right angle by
itself. The first thing it needs is a way to answer the question you were
just answering by eye: _if I launch at this speed and this angle, where does
the ball come down?_

There is a formula for that when there is no air. Do not use it. In the last
milestone the air comes back, and the formula stops working. A simulation
keeps working.

### The loop

The ball's state is four numbers: its position `x`, `y` and its velocity
`vx`, `vy`. The launch speed and angle set the starting velocity, split into
components:

$$
v_x = v\cos\theta \qquad\qquad v_y = v\sin\theta
$$

Then, over and over, for a small time step `dt`:

1. gravity changes the vertical velocity: `vy` goes down by `G * dt`,
2. the velocity moves the ball: `x` goes up by `vx * dt`, and `y` by
   `vy * dt`,
3. if `y` has dropped below zero, the ball has landed.

### Your task

Write the three update lines in `landing_distance`. The program compares your
simulation with the formula for three launches, so you can see that it
agrees.
