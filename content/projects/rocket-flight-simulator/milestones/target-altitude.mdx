You have a working simulator: gravity, thrust, a changing mass and air drag.
The last step is to use it the way engineers do, to answer a question:

**How much fuel does the rocket need to reach 400 metres?**

### No formula to rearrange

With air drag there is no equation you can solve for the fuel. But you do
have a function that tells you how high the rocket goes for any amount of
fuel. And you know one more thing: more fuel goes higher.

That is enough. Pick a range that must contain the answer, say 0 to 2 kg:

1. Try the middle of the range.
2. If the rocket overshoots the target, the answer is in the lower half.
   If it falls short, the answer is in the upper half.
3. Keep that half and repeat.

Every round halves the uncertainty. After 25 rounds it is smaller than a
millionth of a gram. This is the **bisection method**, and it works for any
question of the form "what setting gives this result?", as long as turning
the setting up always pushes the result the same way.

### Seeing the flight

Numbers are easier to trust when you can see them. The starter code plots
altitude against time for the flight you find, with a dashed line at the
target. The curve should just touch it.

<Callout kind="note" title="The first run takes a little longer">

Plotting uses the matplotlib library, which is downloaded the first time you
run this lab. After that it is kept on your device.

</Callout>
