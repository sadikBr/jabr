Here is an equation with no tidy solution:

$$
x^3 - x - 2 = 0
$$

In the Continuity lesson you trapped its root by halving an interval over and
over. That works, but slowly: each step buys one more binary digit. Tangents
can do far better.

**The idea.** Take a guess, $x_0$. It is probably wrong: $f(x_0)$ is not
zero. But you can draw the tangent to the curve there, and a straight line is
easy to solve. Find where the _tangent_ crosses zero, and use that as your
next guess.

The tangent at $x_0$ is $L(x) = f(x_0) + f'(x_0)(x - x_0)$. Setting
$L(x) = 0$ and solving for $x$:

$$
x_1 = x_0 - \frac{f(x_0)}{f'(x_0)}
$$

Then do it again from $x_1$, and again. This is **Newton's method**.

For $\sqrt{2}$, the root of $x^2 - 2$, starting from a guess of 1, the
guesses run

$$
1 \;\to\; 1.5 \;\to\; 1.41667 \;\to\; 1.4142157 \;\to\; 1.4142135624
$$

The number of correct digits roughly _doubles_ with every step. Four steps
from a poor guess, and it is right to ten figures.

### Your task

Complete `newton`: one line inside the loop. The program then solves three
equations and prints every guess along the way, so you can watch the digits
lock in.
